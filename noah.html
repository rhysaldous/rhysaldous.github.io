Did Noah really live to 950?

Biblical lifespans look impossible next to a modern seventy-year life. The text still says what it says.

Also on Medium: original post

Genesis gives numbers that clash with the world we live in. Noah is said to have lived 950 years. Methuselah 969. Adam 930. Today a person is doing well to see 80. So either the figures are literal and something changed, or they were never meant as a modern census, or the way those writers counted a “year” is not ours.

The line from Adam to Noah

The genealogy runs Adam to Seth to Noah. Noah fathers Shem, Ham and Japheth at 500, survives the flood, and lives another 350 years. Shem is given 600 years and, on a plain reading, lives long enough to overlap later patriarchs. That is a world in which a human life is measured in centuries.

What the text itself offers

Genesis 6:3, before the flood: “My Spirit shall not abide in man forever, for he is flesh: his days shall be 120 years.” After the flood the ages fall. Shem 600. By Moses the 120-year cap is the frame people remember. One reading is straightforward: a limit was set.

Other readings reach for a different climate before the flood, or for a genetic “first state” that decayed. Those are theories. They are not lab results. I am not going to dress them as science.

Literal or symbolic

Take it literally and Noah did live 950 years. Take it as ancient honour-language and the number marks stature, the way Sumerian king lists give reigns of tens of thousands of years. Scholars split. I am not going to pretend I settled a fight that is older than English.

Are we sure about 2024 — or 2026?

The Gregorian calendar is late. 1582. Before that, years were lunar, solar, regnal, seasonal. If an early “year” was shorter than ours, the huge ages shrink. If the ages are real as written, then our timeline of the past is the thing that needs the argument. I asked the question in the original piece. I still think it is a fair question. I do not claim a new chronology.

If people lived that long now

Families would stack generations in one room. Resources would be a fight. Careers would last centuries. Culture would either freeze under the old or deepen because people had time. That thought experiment is the point of the essay, not a prediction.

Whether Noah’s 950 years were history, symbol, or a different clock, the text still forces the same habit: do not assume the modern year is the only year that ever existed.
